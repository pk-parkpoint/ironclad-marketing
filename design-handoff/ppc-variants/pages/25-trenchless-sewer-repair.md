## Section 2 — Signs  `data-slot="signs-title"`
- **Heading:** When Trenchless Sewer Repair Makes Sense
- **Intro:** When a camera confirms a damaged sewer line, trenchless repair can restore the pipe without turning the whole yard into a trench.
- **Rows 01–04:**
  1. **Camera shows cracked or offset pipe** — Damage that repeats backups may be repairable through lining or bursting instead of full excavation.
  2. **Roots keep coming back** — Roots that return after clearing often need the pipe sealed or replaced, not just cleaned again.
  3. **You want to protect hardscape** — Driveways, patios, landscaping, and mature yards make low-dig options worth considering.
  4. **The line is aging out** — Clay, cast iron, or failing sewer pipe can sometimes be restored with a new liner or replacement path.

## Section 3 — Services  `data-slot="services-title"`
- **Heading:** Our Trenchless Sewer Repair Services
- **Intro:** We inspect first, compare trenchless and traditional options, then repair the line with the least disruption that still solves the problem.
- **Cards 1–6** (photos: see `PHOTO-LIST.md` → 25. Trenchless sewer repair):
  1. **Cured-in-place pipe lining** — A resin liner creates a new pipe wall inside the old line where conditions allow.
  2. **Pipe bursting** — A new line is pulled through while the failing pipe is split outward.
  3. **Spot lining** — Localized damage repaired without replacing the full run.
  4. **Pre-repair camera inspection** — We confirm pipe condition, depth, length, and whether trenchless is suitable.
  5. **Line cleaning and prep** — Roots, scale, and buildup cleared before lining or replacement.
  6. **Post-repair verification** — Final camera footage confirms the restored line is open and sound.
